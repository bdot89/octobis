# OctoBiS

Phase-by-phase best-in-slot gear lists for [OctoWoW](https://octowow.st), in the spirit of
BisBeard but narrowed to the question that actually matters at the keyboard: **for my class and
spec, in this phase, what should I be wearing, and where do I get it?**

The site is static — plain HTML, CSS and ES modules reading JSON. There is no build step and no
server. A separate C# console app scrapes the OctoWoW database and writes the JSON.

```
index.html              the site entry point (repo root, so ./config and ./data resolve)
site/css, site/js       stylesheet and ES modules
config/                 hand-maintained: phases, stat weights, slots, races, overrides
data/                   generated by the scraper — do not edit by hand
tools/OctoBis.Scraper   the data pipeline
tools/OctoBis.Serve     static file server for local preview
```

## Using it

Create a character (name, class, spec, race — up to eight, saved in your browser), pick a phase from
the Database dropdown, then click any equipment slot to see every item that fits it, ranked best to
worst with a 0–100 score and the boss, vendor or recipe it comes from. **Auto-fill best in slot**
equips the whole set at once, and gears to the hit cap for whatever the loadout fights — bosses for
PvE, players for PvP — counting your talents first, so a Shadow priest with 5/5 Shadow Focus is sent
looking for 6% from gear rather than the full 16%. Change the talents and auto-fill again and the
set changes with them.

Every character keeps **two completely separate loadouts, PvE and PvP**, each with its own gear and
its own talent build. Both appear under the character in the dropdown, and the PvE/PvP toggle in the
top bar switches between them.

Gear is saved per character, per loadout *and per phase*, so switching from Launch to Naxxramas
shows a different set rather than overwriting the first. The **Talents** tab is a full calculator
with vanilla's rules enforced: 51 points, five points per tier, and prerequisites that block both
training and untraining.

### Enchants, buckles and gems

Every slot that can take something carries a line under it. These are **not phase-gated** — anything
you can apply is offered in every phase.

`--attachments` scrapes all three kinds straight from the database: **227 enchants, 28 jewelcrafting
gems and 10 belt buckles**. Gems go on rings and amulets ("Permanently enchant a ring or amulet"),
buckles on the waist, and head/leg enchants — which are reputation items rather than enchanting
recipes — come in by name alongside the rest.

Stats are **parsed from the database's own effect wording**, not hand-curated: a spell page reads
"Permanently enchant gloves to increase agility by 15" and that becomes `{ agi: 15 }`. Anything the
parser cannot read still appears, shows its real effect text, and is marked as not counted.

Proc and timed effects are detected and deliberately left unscored. Crusader reads "increases
Strength by 100 for 15 sec"; taking that literally ranked it the best weapon enchant in the game by
a wide margin, which is exactly the trap the item rankings already avoid.

Weapon and shield enchants filter themselves to what you are holding — a shield enchant only shows
if there is a shield in your off hand, and a two-hander is offered two-handed enchants only.
`config/enchants.json` is an override layer for correcting individual entries.

Enchant values are read from the database's own wording, which is its own dialect and a fragmented
one: vanilla says the same thing a dozen ways — "give 7 Agility", "adds 8 agility", "grant +4 to all
stats", "increase Spell Power by 3" — and each phrasing needs its own pattern. A phrasing with no
pattern behind it does not fail loudly; it produces an enchant worth nothing, which looks exactly
like an enchant you chose badly. Every wording that appears in the data is covered by a test, and
the run prints how many effects it could not read.

Patterns accumulate, so two that match one sentence both add their value. That doubled three armour
enchants once, and one of the culprits was a bare-number rule matching the "25" inside "125 armor".
The overlap-prone sentences are pinned by tests of their own.

### How auto-fill handles hit

Hit cannot be ranked like an ordinary stat. It is a requirement with a hard ceiling: below the cap
you are missing attacks outright, above it every point is dead. A weight high enough to reach the cap
overvalues hit in every other slot, and a weight low enough to be fair leaves the set short. So the
cap is treated as a constraint and the set is built in three passes:

1. **Greedy** — the best item per slot, with hit past the cap valued at nothing.
2. **Gap pass** — buy the hit still missing, cheapest first, ranked by score given up per point of
   hit gained. Hit beyond the cap is not what is being bought and does not count towards the trade.
3. **Refine** — re-optimise every slot knowing the whole set, forbidden from dropping below the hit
   the gap pass secured.

Talents and enchants are counted before a single item is chosen, so the gear only shops for the
remainder. Where the cap cannot be reached at all — 16% spell hit does not exist in Launch-phase
gear — the second pass reports what it actually managed, and the third optimises around that instead
of throwing away good gear chasing a number that is not there.

Verified across all 448 class/spec/phase/loadout combinations, each built twice — once with no
talents and once with every applicable hit talent maxed. Every set reaches its cap, and hit talents
never increase what gear has to find: they reduce it in 348 of the 384 builds that have any, and the
36 that do not are cases where the winning items carry hit for free.

### Tooltips

Hovering an item shows the tooltip the game shows, in the game's order: name in its quality colour,
binding, slot and type, weapon damage and speed, armour, stats, durability, class and level
restrictions, then the green `Equip:` / `Use:` / `Chance on hit:` lines.

Those green lines matter more than they look. The ranker turns "Equip: Increases damage and healing
done by magical spells and effects by up to 20" into `spellPower: 20`, and a tooltip built from the
parsed numbers alone would show `+20 SP` — losing the wording for every proc and on-use effect,
which have no number at all. So the sentences are kept alongside the numbers and printed verbatim.
Anything the stat parser could not read is printed too, so a tooltip never quietly omits part of an
item.

Set pieces get the full block: every piece in the set, the ones you are already wearing in white and
the rest greyed out, and each bonus turning green as you reach its threshold. Below that — this part
is not in a game tooltip — is where the item drops, since that is the question the site exists to
answer.

Effect sentences repeat heavily across the database, so `items.json` stores each distinct one once
and items reference it by index; set blocks are stored once per set rather than on all eight pieces.
Without that the file would be 1.5 MB instead of 1.1 MB. Gzipped, the whole tooltip feature costs
about 22 KB on the wire.

### The Guide tab

Hit is the one stat with a hard ceiling: below the cap it is usually a spec's best stat, and one
point above it is worth exactly nothing. The guide shows what you need against players, ordinary
mobs and level 63 bosses, and what your gear and talents actually give you.

Each target leads with **what gear has to supply**, not the raw cap, because that is the number you
shop against. A Shadow priest's spell hit cap is 16%, but five points in Shadow Focus cover 10% of
it, so the card says *"Gear must supply 6%"* and shows the 16%/10% split underneath. Quoting the
16% at a specced character would just be misleading.

Talent hit is read from your real build, so respeccing changes the answer — and it is school-aware,
so a Shadow priest's Shadow Focus counts while a Holy priest's identical points do not. Gear,
enchants and talents are broken out separately so you can see where the number comes from.

**Weapon skill is a toggle**, because OctoWoW grants +5 per weapon type through the Fray Island
quest chain (Iron Determination at level 55+, then Sakgoth). That matters more than it looks: vanilla
applies a much steeper miss penalty once a target's defense exceeds your weapon skill by more than
10, so those five points take the boss melee cap from **9% down to 6%** — worth more than any single
item. Against players it buys nothing, since you are already at the floor.

When you are **over** the cap it names the pieces wasting it and the best replacement for each,
scoring surplus hit as worthless so the comparison reflects what the item is actually worth to you:

> **Legs +72** — replace Titanic Leggings with Legplates of Carnage.
> Titanic Leggings wastes 2% hit above the 9% cap. Swapping changes hit by −2% and trades it for
> +2% crit, +19 Strength, −10 Agility, +6 Stamina.

When you are **under** it, it lists the slot-by-slot upgrades that supply the most hit, with where
they drop.

Two other views cover the same data differently: **BiS List** is the flat table with ranked
alternatives per slot, and **Obtain Checklist** regroups the picks by instance so you can see what a
given raid night is worth.

## Running it

Preview the site:

```bash
dotnet run --project tools/OctoBis.Serve
```

Then open <http://localhost:5080>.

Re-scrape the data:

```bash
dotnet run --project tools/OctoBis.Scraper
```

Useful flags:

| Flag | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| `--zone "Blackwing Lair"` | Restrict the crawl to one instance. Verification runs take minutes instead of hours. |
| `--limit 50` | Cap pages per round. Smoke tests. |
| `--delay 400` | Milliseconds between requests (default 800). |
| `--refresh` | Clear the cache and refetch everything. |
| `--download-icons` | Save icons locally instead of hotlinking the database. |
| `--cache <path>` | Move the page cache (default `%LOCALAPPDATA%\OctoBiS\cache`). |
| `--inspect-zone <id>` | Print the spawn seed for a zone and exit. |
| `--inspect-npc <id>` | Print every listview the parser sees on an NPC page. |
| `--inspect-item <id>` | Print parsed stats, sources and unmatched tooltip lines for an item. |
| `--atlas <path>` | Point at the Atlas-CFM addon folder (auto-detected in a normal WoW install). |
| `--atlas-report <path>` | List every instance Atlas knows, with encounter and loot counts. No network. |
| `--atlas-report <path> --detail <key>` | Dump one instance's encounters, loot, drop rates and tokens. |
| `--talents` | Scrape the talent trees into `data/talents.json`. Run on its own; nothing else needs it. |

The scraper is deliberately single-threaded with a delay between requests, and caches every page to
disk. OctoWoW is volunteer-run; please do not parallelise it.

## How the data is gathered

Two sources, each doing what it is good at:

- **[Atlas-CFM](https://github.com/paokkerkir)** — the in-game loot browser addon. Its database is
  curated for this server family and is the authority on *sources*: which encounter drops what, at
  what rate, in which instance, plus crafted gear and reputation rewards. Crucially it keeps each
  instance separate, including the custom ones the website's zone data cannot tell apart.
- **The OctoWoW database** — the authority on *item stats*, which Atlas does not carry.

Point the scraper at the addon with `--atlas <path>`; it also looks in the usual WoW install
location automatically. Without it the crawl still runs on website data alone, just less accurately.

Atlas entries are tagged with the servers they apply to. OctoWoW runs client 1.18.1, which Atlas
identifies as the "Turtle WoW" profile — that profile inherits content marked 1.17.2 and excludes
Vanilla-Plus-only entries. `AtlasImporter` implements that rule.

### Reading the addon

The data files are Lua source, not a data format: comments, local variables, `unpack()` splices,
and expressions like `LZ["Blackwing Lair"]`. `LuaTable` reads the subset needed rather than
embedding an interpreter. Localisation lookups resolve to their own keys, which are the English
strings, so no locale files are required.

### Talent trees

`--talents` reads OctoWoW's own talent calculator. It is a Next.js app with no JSON endpoint behind
it — every likely data path 404s — so the trees are pulled out of the React Server Component payload
embedded in each class page. Each node carries its tree, its grid cell (`row × 4 + column`), rank
count, icon and prerequisite; the name and description come from the tooltip that follows it.

Descriptions interpolate per-rank values through nested components, which is why the reader takes
only top-level elements and substitutes a component's `values` array as "1/2/3". A handful of
talents defer their value to a separate payload chunk; those render a `?` rather than a wrong number.

### Website crawl

The website still supplies stats, and its own loot data is kept as a supplement for anything Atlas
does not mention. That crawl has no bulk export and no usable GET filter API, so it works in rounds:

1. **Seed** — zone-to-NPC spawn data comes from
   [pfQuest-octo](https://github.com/paokkerkir/pfQuest-octo), which ships the server's spawn tables
   as Lua. `config/phases.json` mostly names zones, not bosses.
2. **Loot** — each seeded NPC's page yields its drop table and vendor stock.
3. **Detail** — each candidate item's page yields its stats and its *complete* source list, which
   reveals bosses that had no spawn entry to seed from.
4. **Backfill** — those newly revealed NPCs are crawled in turn.

Rounds 3 and 4 are why boss rosters mostly do not have to be maintained by hand, which matters most
for Turtle's custom instances where published boss lists are thin or absent.

Three further sources fill gaps that crawling cannot reach:

- **`Sets.lua`** — 1,901 item ids covering T0, T1, T1VP, T2, T3, T3.5, ZG, AQ20 and AQ40 for every
  class, plus artifacts, legendaries and the world blue and epic lists. This matters because OctoWoW
  ships several variants of each tier set: Priest tier 1 alone is Vestments, Regalia *and* Attire of
  Prophecy, 24 pieces in total, and only the first is reachable from boss loot.
- **Container ids** on loot entries, which link a drop to what it becomes. That is how quest-chain
  rewards arrive — The Eye of Divinity lists `{ 18608, 18609 }`, which is Benediction and Anathema.
  Every id in the list is followed, not just the first.
- **`questItems` in `config/phases.json`** — class quest rewards that nothing at all points to.
  Rhok'delar, Lok'delar, Quel'Serrar and Verigan's Fist are dropped by nobody and sold by nobody, and
  the addon records their quests as prose rather than item ids, so they are named and resolved
  through the database search.

None of these are given a phase directly; each item's own page supplies its real sources, and the
phase follows from those.

### Phase gating

An item is only offered in a phase if it is actually obtainable by then. Getting this wrong is the
worst thing this site can do, so it is worth stating how it works.

**Unknown is a real state.** An item whose phase cannot be established has `minPhase: null` and is
excluded from every phase-gated view. It is emphatically *not* treated as available now — an earlier
version defaulted unknown to launch and put Atiesh, Ring of the Fallen God and Seal of the Damned
into the launch planner. Roughly 320 items sit in this state; `data/meta.json` counts them.

Three things feed a phase, in order of trust:

1. **The item's own sources.** A drop in Blackwing Lair is Blackwing Lair gear.
2. **Its tier set**, via `atlasSetPhases`. A Naxxramas set piece belongs to Naxxramas whether or not
   the crawl found the boss that drops it.
3. **Nothing** — it stays unknown.

Two source kinds needed special handling, because both look launch-available and are not:

- **Quest rewards** below epic quality are taken as available now: a quest ships with the build, and
  the phases here are raid releases. The set fallback runs first, so tier pieces awarded by a quest
  keep their raid's phase — the Cryptstalker set is a quest reward and stays in Naxxramas. Epic and
  legendary quest rewards are excluded from the rule: they are endgame almost by definition, and
  theirs are exactly the quests nothing can place. Without that cut, Atiesh became launch gear and
  101 of the 224 class/spec/phase combinations equipped it.
- **Atlas's crafting tables are recipe *spell* ids**, not item ids, and the namespaces collide:
  spell 23067 is the Blue Firework recipe while item 23067 is Ring of the Cryptstalker. They are
  resolved through `Spells.lua` before use. Reading them directly stapled "Crafted" onto whichever
  item happened to share a number with a recipe — 149 items — and marked each one available from
  launch, which is how a Naxxramas tier ring turned up in the launch planner.
- **Crafted items** are only available when their recipe is, and recipe availability is not in the
  data. Where an item is both craftable and dropped, the drop decides: Yshgo'lar is listed as an
  Engineering craft but its only other source is C'Thun, so it is Ahn'Qiraj gear, not launch gear.
- **Reputation rewards** follow their faction — Zandalar Tribe is Zul'Gurub, Brood of Nozdormu is
  Ahn'Qiraj. See `factionPhases`.

`availableIn()` in `site/js/score.js` is the single gate all views go through. The guarantee is
tested by auto-filling all 224 class/spec/phase combinations and checking every equipped item,
plus every BiS row and slot-picker candidate — 194,718 entries, zero violations.

There is one case discovery cannot reach, and `phases.json` has a `npcs` list for it: a boss with no
spawn entry *whose loot is exclusive to him*. Nothing else ever mentions him, so nothing can lead to
him. Nefarian is the type case, along with most summoned encounters — Ragnaros, Hakkar, C'Thun,
Kel'Thuzad. Those are named explicitly and resolved through the database search; anything that fails
to resolve is reported in `data/meta.json` rather than disappearing quietly.

### A trap worth knowing about

Item listviews prefix each name with a digit — `2Nemesis Gloves`. It is *not* the item quality.
Nemesis Gloves is an epic and is prefixed `2`; Hero's Buckler is genuinely uncommon and prefixed
`3`. Reading quality from a listview inverts the filter, keeping green world-drops and discarding
raid epics. Quality is only trustworthy from the item page's own tooltip, and is therefore applied
*after* a page is fetched, never before. `ListviewParserTests` pins this down.

### What the crawl deliberately skips

- **Open-world zones.** Seeding a zone crawls every NPC in it, and somewhere like Winterspring is
  thousands of pages of trash that drops nothing a level 60 would slot. Named world bosses are
  listed individually instead.
- **World-drop greens and blues.** An item on hundreds of loot tables takes the database roughly
  15 seconds to render, against 0.4s for an ordinary item. They are recognisable before fetching by
  their drop rate — a world drop lands around 1.3% off any given mob, real boss loot at 6%+ — so
  anything below 2% that is not an epic is skipped. This is the single biggest saving in the crawl.

## How rankings are decided

There are no published BiS lists for OctoWoW to copy, so items are scored from per-spec stat
weights in `config/specs.json`:

```
score = Σ (stat value × weight), with caps applied
      + any bonus from config/overrides.json
```

Weights are relative, not absolute — the primary throughput stat is 1.0 and everything else is
expressed against it. Scoring happens in the browser, so editing a weight and reloading re-ranks
the whole site without re-scraping.

`config/overrides.json` is the editorial layer for everything the maths cannot see: set bonuses,
proc and on-use effects, weapon-speed breakpoints. It can pin an item to a slot, exclude one
entirely, add a score bonus, or attach a note. Anything carrying an override is badged **adjusted**
in the UI so the reasoning stays visible.

## Known limitations

Please read these before treating any list as authoritative.

- **Set bonuses are approximate.** They are modelled as a flat per-item score bonus, not as a true
  set-level optimisation, so tier breakpoints will be somewhat wrong until an override is added.
  The bonus text is parsed off the item tooltips and shown on hover, but it is not scored.
- **280 tier pieces belong to no set the database knows about**, so they show no set block at all.
  The database registers 88 item sets covering 514 pieces (its own `?itemsets` index is the
  authority, and it is what these numbers come from). OctoWoW's per-spec tier variants are not among
  them: Cenarion Regalia and Cenarion Harness exist in the game alongside vanilla's Cenarion
  Raiment, but only Cenarion Raiment is a registered set. The same is true of Nemesis Attire,
  Felheart Attire, Judgement Battleplate and the rest of the variant tier sets.

  Nothing available states their bonuses. Not the database, and not Atlas — despite the name,
  `Sets.lua` is a catalogue of item groupings with no bonus text anywhere in the addon. It is also
  laid out for a two-column display, so the entries after a set's header are not all that set's:
  Cenarion Regalia's last four pieces appear below Cenarion Harness's eight, separated by blank
  rows. Deriving membership from it produces sets that are simply wrong, which is worse than
  showing none.

  Add them to `config/overrides.json` if you know the bonuses from in-game.
- **Race/class combinations are vanilla's.** OctoWoW states it opens up combinations beyond vanilla
  but does not publish them in a form worth guessing at, so `config/races.json` uses vanilla's plus
  the two added races. Correct that file if a combination is wrong — nothing else reads race data.
- **Talents feed the hit guide, but not the gear rankings.** The guide reads hit talents from your
  real build. Stat weights, though, are per-spec rather than per-build, so talents do not otherwise
  re-rank gear — doing that properly means modelling what every talent changes, not just the hit
  ones.
- **Weapon skill is modelled at 300 or 305 only.** Those are the two values the Fray Island quest
  produces, and the caps for them are stated in `config/hitcaps.json`. Racial weapon-skill bonuses
  stack past 305 in vanilla; values above the top entry fall off at a flat rate rather than being
  modelled properly. The dual-wield white-hit cap is reported for information only, since gear
  cannot reach it.
- **96 of 265 attachments carry no stats**, and each is deliberate: flat health and mana pools (no
  base values here to add them to), profession skills, movement speed, threat, procs (Crusader,
  Fiery Weapon — unscored for the same reason procs on items are), and damage conditional on the
  target's type. They are applied and shown, labelled as contributing nothing rather than quietly
  scoring zero. `config/enchants.json` can give any of them a value.
- **Crafting skill levels are not shown.** Atlas records four numbers per recipe whose meaning is
  not documented and does not read consistently as "skill required", so only the profession is
  displayed. The numbers are still parsed if someone works out what they are.
- **Auto-fill can still land over the hit cap** when the best gear carries hit regardless. A Naxxramas
  rogue with 5/5 Precision needs 4% from gear but every Bonescythe piece has 1% on it, and each still
  wins its slot with that hit valued at zero. The surplus is real, not the ranker chasing hit; the
  guide's replacement advice reports a swap only when one actually comes out ahead.
- **Proc and on-use effects are not scored at all.** They are captured as notes on the item and
  shown in the tooltip, but contribute nothing to the ranking unless given a bonus in the overrides.
- **Conditional stats are carried but never scored.** Attack power that applies only in a druid form
  or only against one creature type is not attack power you have. Both get their own stat key:
  `apFeral`, which only the two feral specs weight, and `apVsTarget`, which nothing weights. Scored
  as plain attack power they are ruinous — a hunter was handed Atiesh, a caster staff, for its
  "+420 Attack Power in Cat, Bear, and Dire Bear forms only", and 24 further pieces of feral gear
  were inflated the same way for every class that can wear leather.
- **Relic slots come up empty** for Paladin, Shaman and Druid. Librams, totems and idols carry no
  stats at all — they modify abilities — so there is nothing for a stat-weight engine to rank. The
  slot says so rather than picking arbitrarily; give the good ones a bonus in `overrides.json` to
  rank them.
- **Unreleased phases reflect today's database.** Loot tables for content that has not shipped are
  whatever the server holds now and may still change.
- **Phase 5 (Timbermaw Hold) is empty.** Atlas knows the raid — 11 encounters, 143 items — but the
  OctoWoW database returns no tooltip for any of those item ids, so there are no stats to rank.
  That is unreleased content, and it will populate on its own. `data/meta.json` lists the count per
  instance under `atlas.itemsMissingFromDatabase`, which is the quickest way to see what the server
  has not shipped yet.
- **Drop rates are inherited from the OctoWoW database** and are only as good as it is; the
  server's own forum has threads about its accuracy.
- **Stat weights are a starting point.** They are seeded from established vanilla theorycraft, not
  from simulation against OctoWoW's own mechanics.

## Data provenance

- Boss, drop-rate, crafting and reputation data: the Atlas-CFM addon's own loot database, read from
  a local install of the addon
- Talent trees: [OctoWoW's talent calculator](https://octowow.st/talents/)
- Item stats: [OctoWoW database](https://octowow.st/db/)
- Phase schedule: [OctoWoW roadmap](https://octowow.st/roadmap) — the roadmap states dates are
  subject to change, so they live in `config/phases.json` and are never hardcoded in markup
- Spawn and zone data: [pfQuest-octo](https://github.com/paokkerkir/pfQuest-octo)
- Item icons are hotlinked from the OctoWoW database; run the scraper with `--download-icons` for a
  fully self-contained deployment

## Deploying

Live at **<https://bdot89.github.io/octobis/>**, served by GitHub Pages from the repository root on
`master`. Pushing to `master` redeploys it; the build takes well under a minute.

`index.html` and everything it references are static, so any other static host works the same way —
drop `index.html`, `site/`, `config/` and `data/` in together. `tools/` is the data pipeline and is
not needed to serve the site. A `.nojekyll` file stops Pages running the tree through Jekyll.

Two things worth knowing before sharing the link widely:

- The dataset is downloaded on first load, currently about 2.4MB.
- Item icons are hotlinked from the OctoWoW database, so each visitor's page load touches their
  server. Run the scraper with `--download-icons` to bundle them instead.
